## User story
As an owner, I want the two destructive actions separated and explained, so that I know which one I am about to do.

## UX flow (LabelDesk words)
1. **Settings → Workspace → `Danger zone`** — two bordered blocks, each with a heading that says what it destroys:
   `Delete this workspace` (`Deletes every project, version, model, stream and key in “Site A”. Members lose access.`)
   and `Delete my account` (`Deletes your account and any workspace you own alone.`).
2. **Delete this workspace** — `Delete workspace` (danger) opens a dialog with the exact-slug field
   (`Type site-a to confirm`), a checklist of what goes, and `Delete workspace` disabled until the slug matches. A
   `You cannot undo this.` line sits above the buttons.
3. **Policy states** — on the default workspace the block reads `This is your default workspace and cannot be deleted.`
   with no button; on enterprise, `Enterprise workspaces are deleted by LabelDesk support.`; a non-owner sees the block
   read-only with a lock and `Request access`.
4. **Delete my account** — a dialog with password and exact-email fields; a `409` shows the blocking sentence and links
   the members tab.
5. **`deleting` state** — the block becomes a progress line `Deleting… you will be signed out when it finishes.` and every
   other settings control is disabled.
6. **`[error]`** a `423` from another tab shows `This workspace is being deleted.` and refreshes the page state.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Two blocks, each stating exactly what it destroys, with separate confirms.
- [ ] The workspace confirm needs the exact slug; the account confirm needs password and email.
- [ ] Default-workspace, enterprise and non-owner states each render their own sentence, not a failing button.
- [ ] Empty, loading and error states above all render.
- [ ] Workspace A cannot see or change workspace B's data (404, not 403, for cross-workspace ids).
- [ ] Flag off ⇒ each block is absent with its own key; both off ⇒ the Workspace tab is unchanged.

## Notes for the implementer
The heading is the contract: "Delete this workspace" never shares a dialog with "Delete my account". A blocked state is a
sentence, never a disabled button with no reason.
