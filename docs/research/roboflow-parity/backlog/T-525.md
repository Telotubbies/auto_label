## User story
As someone on the first-run tour, I want to read the explanation and the card it is about at the same time,
so that the tour teaches me the screen instead of hiding it.

## UX flow (LabelDesk words)
1. **Home, first load** — the tour card sits opposite its target: bottom-left when the target is on the
   right, top-left when the target is low. It never overlaps the ringed card.
2. **Each step** — the page does not scroll under the reader. If the target is already visible, nothing
   moves; if it is not, one short scroll puts it in view without centring it behind the card.
3. **Spotlight** — a 2 px dashed `--ring` at 60% with a 4 px offset, distinguishable from the keyboard
   focus ring at a glance.
4. **Close / Done** — unchanged, including Escape and the once-per-person-per-workspace memory.
   [empty: n/a] [loading: n/a] [error: a step whose target is missing skips to the next step]

## Notes for the implementer
Options considered:
1. **Corner chosen from the target's position (recommended).** Four corners, one pure function, no measuring
   loop, and the ring stays the only thing the eye follows.
2. **Anchor the card to the target's edge (a real popover).** Best result, but it needs collision handling
   and a second layout pass for a five-step tour that runs once.
3. **Move the card to the top centre.** Simplest, but on a 900 px screen it lands on the header the first
   step is about.

The ring is the one thing this card may change in `index.css`. Do not touch `--ring`, `--glass-*` or the
focus rule: the focus ring must stay the strongest outline on the page.
