## User story
As the person who wrote the three-class brief, I want to see what else is in my images with a crop and a count, and either promote or dismiss it in one click, so that the dataset's blind spots become visible before the model learns to ignore them.

## UX flow (LabelDesk words)
1. **Dataset toolbar → Discover objects** (`/:ws/:project/browse`) — a button that opens a panel with the sample size (50 / 200 / 500) and the cost sentence. [empty: "No sweep yet — reading 50 images is enough to start"] [error: "no open-vocabulary model is available on this worker" with the models link]
2. **Running** — `[loading: "Reading 120 of 200 images…" with the progress bar]`; the panel stays open and the grid behind it stays usable.
3. **The list** — one row per phrase: crop thumbnail, phrase, count, "seen in N images", an overlap badge ("similar to Forklift truck"), a tick box, and "Ignore". Ignored rows sink to a muted section.
4. **Actions** — "Add 3 as classes" creates them with the phrase as name; the panel confirms and links to Classes & Tags. "Ignore 1" removes it from the list and from later sweeps.
5. **A second sweep** — rows new since the previous run carry a "new" badge.
6. **Flag off** — no button, no panel.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The panel runs a sweep at 50, 200 or 500 images, states the cost first and shows progress while it runs.
- [ ] Rows are ranked with a crop, a count and an overlap badge; promoted phrases become classes and ignored ones disappear from later sweeps.
- [ ] A repeat sweep marks new phrases without reordering the existing ones unexpectedly.
- [ ] Empty, loading and error states above all render, including no model available and no sweep yet.
- [ ] Workspace A cannot see workspace B's phrases or crops: every row comes from this project's sweep response.
- [ ] Flag off ⇒ no button, no panel and the dataset toolbar is exactly as today.

## Notes for the implementer
The crop is the evidence: never list a phrase without one, and open the crop at full size on click. Promotion is named by the phrase, not by a suggestion — a wrong class name here becomes a wrong ontology everywhere downstream. Keep the panel one screen tall with its own scroll; the grid is what people came for.
